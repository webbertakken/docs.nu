LiveDoc is a web app where people keep **documents of every kind, together, in real time**: pages, diagrams, whiteboards, boards, sheets and forms. It is a multiplayer workspace in the browser: anyone with access to a document can join, see other collaborators' cursors and edits live, and contribute at the same time. The result is a shared visual artifact, a flowchart, an architecture diagram, a mind map, or a brainstorm board, that a team builds together rather than one person authoring and the rest reviewing.

## A multiplayer canvas

The heart of LiveDoc is the [canvas](/help/canvas/the-canvas/). You drop down shapes, connect them with arrows, drag things around, and the diagram takes shape as you work. When more than one person is in the same document, everyone sees live cursors, who has what selected, and every change as it happens.

- **Shapes and arrows** for flowcharts, system diagrams, and process maps. See [Shapes](/help/palette/shapes/) and [Arrows](/help/palette/arrows/).
- **Mind maps and brainstorms** built from quick, keyboard-driven nodes: Tab adds a child, Enter a sibling. See [Mind Maps](/help/palette/mind-maps/).
- **Real-time presence** so collaborators rarely overwrite each other: an element someone else has selected is locked for everyone else.
- **Tabs** to keep related views of one document in a single place. See [Tabs](/help/tabs/using-tabs/).
- **Modes** for different kinds of work on a tab: [Sketch](/help/canvas/sketch-mode/) for freehand whiteboarding, Structured for shapes and arrows, and [Pages](/help/canvas/pages-mode/) for laying a tab out as pages (infographics, posters, handouts and articles). A mode is how a tab is worked on, so everyone on the tab shares it. Beside them, [Plan tabs](/help/canvas/plan-tabs/) hold boards of cards you drag through columns, and the [Workshop control](/help/canvas/workshop/) brings timers, votes and polls into any mode for running a session such as a retro or workshop. See [Editor Modes](/help/canvas/editor-modes/).
- **Templates** for retrospectives, Kanban boards, flowcharts and more, so you rarely start from a blank page. See [Templates](/help/canvas/templates/).

## Built for people who think together

LiveDoc is aimed at people who need to think together, visually: engineering teams sketching architecture and sequence flows, product and design teams mapping journeys and information architecture, and cross-functional groups in workshops, planning sessions, and retrospectives. The unit of value is the team, not the single author.

To make that frictionless, the canvas **always works without signing in**. You can land on a blank diagram, build something real, and hand the link to a colleague who edits it straight away, no account required. See [Sharing your document](/help/getting-started/sharing-your-document/).

## Free and open source

LiveDoc is **MIT-licensed** and **free for everyone**. There is no paid tier, and no feature will ever sit behind a plan. A free hosted version runs at docs.nu, and because the whole codebase is open, anyone can self-host it on their own infrastructure. Every feature is available to every user. See [Why use LiveDoc?](/help/about/why-livedoc/).
