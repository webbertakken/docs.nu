A document is not a single canvas. It is a collection of **tabs**, each with its own canvas, elements, layout, theme, and background. A new document usually starts with one tab, and you add more whenever a piece of work deserves its own space. The tabs live at the bottom right of the editor, in the document group: the active tab, which fills the canvas, then the tab stack, which opens the [tab list](/help/user-interface/tab-bar/) of every tab.

## What a tab is

Each tab is a self-contained canvas inside the document. A tab carries its own elements, its own theme and background pattern, and an optional lock, all independent of the other tabs around it. Common ways people split work across tabs:

- An **Overview** tab with the high-level picture, and detail tabs behind it.
- One tab per system, team, or phase of a project.
- A scratch tab for ideas you are not ready to file yet.

Each tab also has its own [mode](/help/canvas/editor-modes/) (Sketch, Structured or Pages), the same for everyone on it, chosen on its start while it is empty; boards, sheets and dashboards are [Plan tabs](/help/canvas/plan-tabs/) of their own. The small icon at the start of a tab's name, on the active tab and in the tab list, shows it.

## Adding a tab

Press the **+** button at the end of the document group, bottom right, and choose **Tab** in the add menu, or **New Tab** in the tab list, or run **Create New Tab** from the [command palette](/help/search-panel/the-search-panel/command-palette/) (`Cmd` / `Ctrl` + `K`). The same menu, and the tab list's **New Board**, **New Sheet** and **New Dashboard**, add a [Plan tab](/help/canvas/plan-tabs/) instead. The new tab:

- is named `Tab 2`, `Tab 3` and so on, after the number of tabs in the document;
- takes the theme, background and font of the tab you were on, so it matches the work around it;
- opens in the same mode as the tab you were on: made from a Pages tab it is a Pages tab, from a Sketch tab a Sketch tab, and from a Plan tab or an Event Storming board a Structured tab;
- has the [Workshop control](/help/canvas/workshop/) on when the tab you were on has;
- starts on that mode's own start, such as **Start Sketching** or **Start Making Pages**, so you can begin from a template or close it to keep the tab blank.

**Duplicate** in the tab menu makes a copy instead: it is named after the original with "copy" on the end, sits right after it, and is a separate tab with its own elements from then on.

## Naming and reordering tabs

Tabs start with default names like `Tab 1` and `Tab 2`, and you can rename any of them:

- **Click** the active tab's name to edit it inline, or **double-click** a tab's row in the tab list (or press `F2` on it). Enter or clicking away saves, Escape cancels, and an empty name keeps the old one.
- **Rename** in the tab menu (and **Rename Tab** in the command palette) opens the same editor.
- **Auto-rename on first edit:** while a tab still has its default name, editing the label of its first element renames the tab to match. This only happens once, so later edits leave a renamed tab alone.

Tab names are capped at **60 characters**. Runs of spaces and line breaks collapse to one, and anything longer is cut at the last whole word with an ellipsis, so a paragraph typed into a sticky can't turn into an unreadable tab.

In the tab list, drag a tab's row up or down to reorder it. A caret shows where it will land: drop on the upper half of a row to land before it, the lower half to land after it. Reordering only moves the tab in the list, it never touches the content on any tab.

Drag a row out of the tab list to the **right edge** of the screen instead, and it opens beside the tab you're on: see [Side by Side Tabs](/help/tabs/side-by-side/).

## The tab menu

Click the **⋯** button on the active tab, or right-click the active tab or any row in the tab list (which switches to it first), to open its menu. Right-clicking an empty spot on the canvas opens the same menu, with **Paste** moved to a row of its own at the top. On a phone it opens as a sheet from the bottom of the screen.

Along the top sits a row of quick actions: **Rename**, **Duplicate**, **Paste** (greyed out until you have copied something), **Side by Side** (on a large enough window; **Close Side by Side** while two tabs are open), **Lock Tab** and **Delete**. Below them, everything else is a full-width row, in groups split by a line:

- **Add to Folder** and **Add to Document**.
- **Import**, **Export**, and **Clear**, which removes every element from the tab after a confirmation (undo brings them back).

To tidy a tab's layout, search for **Auto Layout** or **Auto-align** in the [command palette](/help/search-panel/the-search-panel/command-palette/). See [Cleaning Up a Tab](/help/tabs/tab-cleanup/).

The [session tools](/help/collaboration/session-tools/) (timer, vote and poll) are not in this menu: they have their own buttons in the tools group, at the bottom left.

## Deleting a tab

Choose **Delete** in the tab menu. The tab goes straight away, and **Undo** brings it back. Once you reload or leave the document it is gone, unless another document also holds it ([Add to Document](/help/tabs/add-to-document/)), so export or duplicate anything you might want later. A few things to know:

- Delete asks first only when it does something Undo can't take back: a [form](/help/canvas/plan-tabs/forms/)'s tab deletes the form's link with it, and a board or sheet that forms send their responses to stops them taking responses.

- A document always keeps at least one tab, so Delete is greyed out on the last one.
- A [locked tab](/help/tabs/locking-tabs/) can't be deleted until you unlock it.
- Links on other tabs that pointed at the deleted tab are removed with it.
- If the tab is also in [another document](/help/tabs/add-to-document/), it is only removed from this one.

## Share links

On a share link for a Viewer or a Participant, and on any link scoped to one tab, you can still click between tabs, but the **+** button, the tab menu, renaming and drag-to-reorder are all hidden. Only an Editor with a link to the whole document changes its tabs.

## More with tabs

Each tab also carries its own menu of actions, covered in their own guides:

- **[Tab Folders](/help/tabs/tab-folders/)** group related tabs under one named heading so a long tab list stays readable.
- **[Side by Side Tabs](/help/tabs/side-by-side/)** puts two tabs on screen at once, each live, with the toolbars following you between them.
- **[Linking Across Tabs](/help/tabs/linking-tabs/)** turns any element into a jump point to another tab.
- **[Add a Tab to Another Document](/help/tabs/add-to-document/)** shares a tab into a different document: it is the same tab in both places, not a copy, so edits appear everywhere it lives.
- **[Importing](/help/tabs/import-tabs/)** and **[Exporting](/help/tabs/export-tabs/)** move a tab's contents in and out as a file.
- **[Locking a Tab](/help/tabs/locking-tabs/)** makes a whole canvas read-only.
- **[Cleaning Up a Tab](/help/tabs/tab-cleanup/)** tidies a tab's layout in one click.

<Tip>
  A shared reference tab such as a glossary can live in more than one document at once with [Add to
  Document](/help/tabs/add-to-document/). Editing it in one place updates it everywhere.
</Tip>
